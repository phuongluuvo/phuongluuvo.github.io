Prethesis, thesis, and internship projects – how to work with me
Please read this page before emailing me

I receive many more requests than I have places. Emails that follow the checklist on this page get a reply; one-line emails asking “please give me a topic” usually do not. This is not personal – it is simply that I cannot evaluate a request without knowing who you are and what you want to work on.

1. What I look for

  • Curiosity and persistence. You should enjoy finding out why something does not work.

  • Comfort with mathematics. Linear algebra, probability, and basic optimization. You do not need to be an expert – you need to be willing to become one.

  • Programming. Python (NumPy/SciPy) or MATLAB for simulations. Git for version control.

  • Reading. You will read research papers, and you will write summaries of them.

  • Reliability. Attending meetings, meeting agreed deadlines, and telling me early when something is stuck.

2. The prethesis / thesis process

  • Step 1 – Choose a topic. Pick one of the topics in section 3 below, or propose your own related to the group's work.

  • Step 2 – Talk to me. Send the email described in section 4. If we agree, we fix the scope in writing (one page: goal, deliverables, timeline).

  • Step 3 – Prethesis (one semester). Literature review, a concrete formulation, and a first simulation or proof-of-concept. Deliverable: a short technical report.

  • Step 4 – Thesis (one to two semesters). Full development, experiments, and a written thesis. Deliverable: thesis document and, if the results allow, a conference or journal paper.

  • Step 5 – Presentation and defence. A group seminar before submission, then the formal defence.

3. Currently available topics

The list below is updated every semester. Topics marked (open) are available now.

T1. Learning-based power control for interference-limited networks (open)

Question: can a learned policy approach the performance of iterative optimization while running in real time?
Scope: reproduce a baseline weighted-MMSE scheme, then train a graph neural network policy on random network instances and compare sum-rate and runtime.
Prerequisites: Python, PyTorch (or willingness to learn), basic probability.
Good outcome: a reproducible simulation study, and a comparison that is honest about where learning wins and where it does not.

T2. Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces with imperfect channel knowledge (open)

Question: how much of the theoretical gain of an RIS survives when the channel is only known approximately?
Scope: formulate a robust beamforming problem, derive a tractable approximation, and quantify the performance loss as a function of channel estimation error.
Prerequisites: convex optimization basics, MATLAB or Python.
Good outcome: a clear robustness/performance trade-off curve plus a short write-up.

T3. Energy efficiency and delay in dense small-cell networks (open)

Question: what is the price, in energy, of guaranteeing a delay constraint?
Scope: build on queueing models, derive a scheduling policy, and evaluate the energy-delay trade-off by simulation.
Prerequisites: queueing basics, simulation experience.
Good outcome: a policy with a provable guarantee plus simulations.

T4. Propose your own topic (open)

If you have read a paper that excited you, write a one-page proposal: the problem, why it matters, what you would do, and what you would deliver. I am happy to discuss it.

4. How to apply

Send one email to phuong.luuvo@example.edu. Start the subject line with the kind of project you are applying for – Prethesis, Thesis, or Internship – followed by your full name, for example Prethesis - Nguyen Van A.

and include:

  • Your CV (one page is enough).

  • Your transcript (an unofficial PDF is fine).

  • The topic number you are interested in (for example T2), or your own one-page proposal.

  • Two or three sentences on why this topic interests you specifically.

  • Your availability: which semester, and how many hours per week.

5. Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to have published anything? No. Enthusiasm plus solid fundamentals is enough.

Do I need to be in the top of my class? Not necessarily – but you must be able to handle the mathematics in the papers you will read.

Can I work remotely? Partly, but the initial meetings should be in person.

How long until I get a reply? Usually within one or two weeks during term time. If you have not heard back after two weeks, a short reminder is welcome.

Can you write me a recommendation letter? Yes, if we have worked together for at least one semester – I can only write about what I have seen.
